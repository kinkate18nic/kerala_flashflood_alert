# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Primary users are informed members of the public in Kerala who want a trustworthy, plain-language view of flood-related risk without having to reconcile multiple official sources themselves. They are often checking conditions quickly on mobile, under uncertain weather, inconsistent bandwidth, or while monitoring a specific district, taluk, or hotspot they care about.

Secondary users are volunteers, field observers, and community-level monitors who need a fast situational picture they can cross-check against field context. They are not necessarily acting as formal responders, but they need enough evidence and transparency to decide where to pay closer attention.

## Product Purpose
Kerala Flash-Flood Watch is a public decision-support dashboard that helps people understand where closer attention is warranted right now for flash-flood, runoff, river-rise, reservoir-release, and dam-related flood consequences in Kerala.

Its purpose is not to replace official agencies or act as a stand-alone warning authority. Its purpose is to make fragmented hazard, hydrology, and consequence signals easier to inspect together, so users can move from vague concern to evidence-linked attention. Success means users can quickly understand what is being highlighted, why it is being highlighted, what the current limits are, and when a signal reflects caution versus genuinely elevated concern.

This is also an advocacy surface in the sense that it argues for transparent, inspectable public-risk communication. It should help users feel more informed and more capable, not more panicked.

## Brand Personality
Credible, calm, actionable.

The voice should feel steady, transparent, and serious without sounding bureaucratic or cold. It should avoid hype, theatrics, and false certainty. When the model is uncertain, the interface should say so plainly. When risk is elevated, the interface should guide attention and next checks rather than dramatize the situation.

Emotionally, the product should create trust, clarity, and measured urgency when needed. It should feel like a reliable public utility with strong editorial judgment, not a marketing surface and not an alarm engine.

## Anti-references
- Government PDF portal
- Generic SaaS dashboard
- Alarmist disaster app
- Newsfeed clone

The experience should not feel document-heavy, cluttered, or visually inert like a legacy public portal. It should not borrow interchangeable enterprise-dashboard tropes that make everything feel abstract and corporate. It should not use flashing urgency, red-heavy panic language, or crisis aesthetics that overstate weak signals. It should also avoid collapsing into a stream of updates that reads like a news product instead of a decision-support tool.

## Design Principles
- Make evidence inspectable: every highlighted area should clearly show the signals, reasoning, and caveats behind it.
- Preserve calm under uncertainty: uncertainty should be visible, but never visualized in a way that increases panic or confusion.
- Guide attention, do not overwhelm it: help users identify what matters now without forcing them to parse every source card or methodology detail first.
- Treat accessibility as operational quality: readability, motion restraint, mobile usability, and color-safe risk communication are part of correctness, not polish.
- Work like a public utility, not a product demo: the interface should earn trust through clarity, restraint, and consistency rather than novelty or visual spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Minimum target is WCAG 2.1 AA.

Reduced motion is a must-have. Low-bandwidth behavior and mobile-first usability are non-negotiable. Risk communication must be color-blind-safe and never rely on hue alone to distinguish levels or states.

The product should prioritize clear text hierarchy, plain-language labels, strong contrast, predictable interactions, and layouts that remain understandable on smaller screens. Important status, confidence, and source-health information should be available even when assets are slow or unavailable.
